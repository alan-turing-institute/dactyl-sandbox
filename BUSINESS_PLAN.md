# Dactyl business plan

## Positioning

Dactyl is a gentle task pond for people whose TODO lists have teeth: a practical task manager wrapped in low-shame, fish-themed rituals that make task review feel less punitive and easier to resume.

For teams, the sharper line is: **lightweight project tracking for teams who prefer ponds to Jira.**

## Thesis

Most productivity tools optimise for managers, hierarchy, and power-user efficiency. Dactyl should optimise for overwhelmed people and small teams who need structure without turning overdue work into a shame pile.

The fish theme is not just decoration. It should earn its keep by improving:

- activation: first task, first completion, first return visit;
- re-entry: coming back after stale or overdue tasks;
- retention: daily/weekly rituals that feel friendly instead of judgemental;
- differentiation: a memorable product identity in a crowded TODO market.

## Target audiences

### 1. Neurodivergent, ADHD, and overwhelmed productivity users

Pain points:

- conventional TODO apps feel judgemental, boring, or easy to abandon;
- overdue tasks can create shame spirals;
- re-entry often matters more than perfect planning.

Dactyl hooks:

- Daily Catch: three to five suggested tasks;
- Ghost net: stale-task recovery without shame;
- focus fish: one active task at a time;
- streaks based on re-engagement, not perfection.

### 2. Students, PhD researchers, and early-career academics

Pain points:

- fragmented reading, writing, admin, deadlines, supervisor asks, and GitHub issues;
- Jira is too heavy and notes apps are too loose.

Dactyl hooks:

- reading/writing/admin task templates;
- project ponds for dissertation or lab work;
- exportable weekly progress summaries.

### 3. Cozy productivity consumers

Adjacent behaviours include Finch, Forest, Habitica, Spirit City, cozy games, and Notion templates.

Dactyl hooks:

- pond customisation;
- gentle reminders;
- recurring rituals;
- tasteful premium pond/fish themes.

### 4. Small playful technical teams

Pain points:

- Jira is too heavy;
- Trello/Notion can drift;
- GitHub issues do not always capture day-to-day planning.

Dactyl hooks:

- shared ponds;
- GitHub import/sync;
- Slack digests;
- stand-up reports;
- read-only planning snapshots.

### 5. Hackweeks, workshops, and education

Pain points:

- short-lived teams need simple planning fast;
- organisers need progress visibility and end-of-event summaries.

Dactyl hooks:

- event ponds;
- team templates;
- public showcase/export;
- short-term hosted workspaces.

## Business model

Recommended starting model: freemium individual product with team and institutional upsell.

### Free

- individual task pond;
- basic auth/sync;
- limited history and customisation.

### Pro individual: £3-6/user/month

- unlimited tasks/history;
- recurring tasks;
- reminders;
- custom views;
- advanced focus mode;
- backup/export;
- premium pond themes.

### Team: £6-12/user/month

- shared ponds;
- reports and stand-ups;
- GitHub integrations;
- Slack/email digests;
- roles and activity history.

### Institution or lab licence: £500-5,000/year

- research groups, RSE teams, and teaching teams;
- procurement-friendly annual billing;
- optional self-hosting/support tier later.

### Event package: £250-1,500/event

- branded hackweek/workshop pond;
- templates and reporting;
- short-term hosted workspace.

## Revenue scenarios

These are annual recurring revenue sketches, not profit forecasts.

| Scenario | Inputs | ARR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny but validated | 1,000 Pro users at £4/month + 10 teams of 6 at £8/month | ~£54k |
| Indie-sustainable niche | 5,000 Pro users at £5/month + 50 teams of 8 at £8/month + 10 institution licences at £1.5k/year | ~£353k |
| Angel-scale opportunity | 25,000 Pro users at £5/month + 250 teams of 10 at £9/month + 50 institution/event accounts at £2k/year | ~£1.87m |
| Venture-scale stretch | 150,000 Pro users at £5/month + 2,000 teams of 10 at £10/month + 500 institution/event accounts at £2k/year | ~£12.4m |

The venture-scale case needs evidence that Dactyl is a broader category product rather than only a niche novelty. The angel/indie path is more credible until activation, retention, and paid conversion data exist.

## Go-to-market plan

### Phase 1: validate the emotional-productivity thesis

Goal: prove the fish theme improves activation and retention.

Actions:

- create a landing page for gentle task pond positioning;
- interview 20-30 ADHD, cozy productivity, student, and research users;
- test willingness to pay for Pro features;
- instrument first task, first completion, D3 return, D30 retention, and stale-task recovery.

Success signals:

- users describe the tone as motivating or less shameful;
- 20%+ landing-page signup conversion from targeted communities;
- 5%+ paid intent or pre-order conversion from early testers.

### Phase 2: launch a paid beta

Actions:

- Pro Individual subscription at £4-5/month;
- PWA/mobile polish;
- pond customisation, recurring tasks, reminders, Daily Catch, and Ghost net;
- referral/share loops around pond snapshots or weekly wins.

Success signals:

- D30 retention above 20-30% for activated users;
- meaningful daily/weekly active use;
- users pay for sync, reminders, and customisation, not only cosmetics.

### Phase 3: expand to teams and institutions

Actions:

- shared ponds;
- GitHub integration;
- Slack summaries;
- team stand-up reports;
- event/workshop templates.

Success signals:

- 3-5 paid team pilots;
- weekly team report usage;
- institutional buyers understand the value without needing the consumer story.

## Investor narrative

Problem:

- productivity tools are powerful but emotionally hostile;
- many users abandon task systems when they become shame piles;
- small teams need lightweight tracking without Jira-level overhead.

Solution:

- Dactyl reframes task management as caring for a pond: playful, gentle, and practical;
- the theme supports habit formation, re-entry, and retention;
- integrations make the product useful beyond novelty.

Why now:

- increasing acceptance of neurodivergent-friendly tools;
- growth of cozy software and gentle productivity;
- remote and hybrid teams need lightweight rituals;
- AI-assisted summaries and triage can feel supportive rather than managerial.

Moat:

- brand and tone;
- behavioural loops around low-shame task recovery;
- data-informed personal triage;
- community identity;
- team/institution workflows layered on top of consumer adoption.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Cute theme is dismissed as a gimmick | Tie each metaphor to reduced friction or retention; offer fish-light professional mode. |
| Consumer productivity churn is high | Prioritise retention metrics before scaling acquisition; add team and institution revenue. |
| Crowded market | Own the gentle task pond niche rather than generic TODO positioning. |
| Too much theme obscures usability | Test language clarity; keep conventional labels available through accessibility/professional settings. |
| VC scale is hard | Present the angel/seed path first; prove retention and paid conversion before claiming venture scale. |
